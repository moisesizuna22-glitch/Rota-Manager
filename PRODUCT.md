# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: solo couriers/delivery drivers who upload their own route spreadsheet, correct addresses, and drive the result — often on a phone, one-handed, outdoors, sometimes mid-route. Secondary: the operator (owner or a small ops team) who uses the separate admin panel to manage accounts, plans, and trials. These are two different working contexts, not one audience wearing two hats.

## Product Purpose

Rota Manager turns a messy delivery spreadsheet into a processed, geocoded route, but its deeper purpose is compounding: every address a courier corrects feeds a shared coordinate bank, so routes get more accurate for every future user, not just the one who fixed it. Gamification (XP, levels, badges) exists to make that correction work feel like progress instead of an unpaid chore. Success is a driver who gets a clean route with minimal manual fixing, and a database that's measurably better than it was last week.

## Positioning

The route tool that gets smarter every time a courier uses it — corrected addresses aren't thrown away after one trip, they make the next route better for everyone.

## Brand Personality

Rugged and game-like: it leans into the RPG leveling metaphor already built into the product (pixel avatars, XP bars, badges, item drops) with real texture and craft. That said, it has to stay grounded — this is a tool people trust with their actual job, not a mobile game skin. Personality shows up as earned progression, not decoration.

## Anti-references

Not a childish or cartoonish mobile game — cutesy gamification would undercut trust in a work tool. Also not a generic corporate SaaS dashboard; the RPG metaphor is the brand's actual differentiator and shouldn't be flattened into sterile enterprise chrome.

## Design Principles

- **Earn, don't decorate.** XP, levels, and badges reward real work (a corrected address, a completed route) — never cosmetic-only flourishes disconnected from what the courier did.
- **Legible in a driver's hand, not just at a desk.** Every screen a courier touches has to hold up one-handed, in motion, in direct sunlight — high contrast and large targets aren't an accessibility add-on here, they're baseline.
- **Make the compounding visible.** The product's real value (a shared database that improves over time) is invisible by default; surface it so a courier can see their correction mattered beyond their own session.
- **Two audiences, two speeds.** The courier's flow is fast, mobile-first, low-friction. The admin's flow is dense and control-oriented. Don't let one leak into the other's layout or pacing.
- **Grounded fantasy, not cartoon.** Lean into the RPG metaphor's texture (pixel art, item rarity, level titles) — never its cuteness.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Outdoor/mobile use is the priority: high-contrast color choices for sunlight readability, and large tap targets for one-handed or gloved use. No specific WCAG level was named beyond that baseline.
